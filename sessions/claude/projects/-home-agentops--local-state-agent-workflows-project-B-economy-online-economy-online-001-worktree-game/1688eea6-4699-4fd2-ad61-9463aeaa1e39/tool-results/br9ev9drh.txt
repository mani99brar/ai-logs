## 3 The sea and islands

Use a top-down tile world with a fixed navigable layout and authored island templates. Randomize their contents and approved placements. Avoid unrestricted procedural geography in the first release: travel fairness and recognizable landmarks are more valuable than unlimited map variation.

| Element | Initial content and behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Sea | Connected routes, shoals as obstacles, clear docking areas |
| Islands | 12 islands drawn from tropical, volcanic, ruin, and mist themes |
| Final island | One of several validated hidden sockets; reachable by every ship |
| Ruins | One globally claimable rubbing source on each regular island |
| Other loot | Fruit pedestals, supply chests, rumor signs, and gold caches |
| Landmarks | Visible silhouettes that help players navigate without opening a map |
| Hazards | Clearly marked slow zones; no lethal environmental traps in version one |

### Movement and visibility

Sailing uses acceleration, braking, and turning, with generous handling rather than a naval simulation. Nearby ships and islands are visible inside a defined sight radius. Unvisited territory is covered by fog on the minimap. Once discovered, terrain stays on the personal map, while enemies disappear outside current visibility.

World positions are authoritative on the server. Island discovery, enemy visibility, and rumors are evaluated there. The client receives only the hidden-state information it is entitled to know. Public terrain can be shared; objective locations and the final-island choice must not be recoverable from a public random seed.

### Exploration activities

Each ruin uses one of two short interactions: a three-symbol sequence or a small navigation puzzle. Reading nearby clues solves it; no external One Piece knowledge is required. Completing it permits a six-second rubbing interaction that can be interrupted by a valid encounter. Total time at a fresh ruin should be about 20–40 seconds.

Rumors identify an island or sector containing a type the player lacks. Stale rumors are visibly marked after their target is claimed. The journal offers a replacement lead from remaining sources or a coarse carrier sighting. It never guarantees that the destination will remain uncontested.

Target neighboring-island travel at 20–40 seconds and the longest useful crossing at 90 seconds or less. Tune map size around those times. Remove empty stretches before adding more sailing mechanics.

## 11 Economy and comeback rules

Keep the match economy small. Its job is to create route choices and help players recover information, without overwhelming the central rubbing race.

| Source or purchase | Starting value | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting purse | 50 gold | Same for every player |
| Supply cache | 25 gold | One global claim per cache |
| Fresh ruin completion | 40 gold | Once per player per ruin; cannot farm the interaction |
| PvP victory | 20 percent of loser gold, capped at 50 | Transfer existing gold; never mint a kill reward |
| Targeted rumor | 25 gold | Lead to a missing rubbing type |
| Haki focus scroll | 75 gold | Full alpha only; one focus equipped |
| Sail tonic | 30 gold | Plus 10 percent sailing speed for 12 seconds |

Gold transfer rounds down and cannot make a balance negative. Shops are dockside objects, not permanent safe zones. Buying is a brief interaction that can be interrupted. Purchases have an idempotent request ID so retries cannot double-spend.

### Consumables

Carry at most two tonics; only one effect may be active. Tonics do not stack, work only at sea, and cannot be activated while being grappled or in a duel. A tonic does not provide damage, invulnerability, or faster treasure claiming. Losing a duel does not drop the remaining consumables.

### Preventing runaway leaders

The winner steals only one missing type, cannot hoard duplicates, and gains no permanent combat level from kills. Fruit powers and maximum duel health survive defeat. Respawn protection and remote spawn selection prevent immediate repeated attacks. Fighting also costs world time while rivals keep exploring.

When a player loses a rubbing, give them a free fresh rumor for that missing type. If all other copies are carried, show a coarse, delayed carrier sighting rather than an exact continuous tracker. This helps them choose a recovery route without handing them a free replacement.

Suppress gold rewards for repeated victories over the same opponent within three minutes. Rubbing theft still follows normal eligibility. Track suspicious repeated forfeits for review; do not automatically punish players merely because friends often encounter one another in private games.

### Client intent and server decisions

Clients send actions such as move, interact with a target, attempt a grapple, submit combat buttons, choose a theft type, or start a claim. They never send authoritative damage amounts, owned rubbing lists, gold balances, final positions, or a declared victory. The server derives every consequence from validated intent.

Theft selection refers to a server-created duel result and an eligible type; it is not a general transfer endpoint. Duplicate, stale, unauthorized, or already-resolved requests return the current result without mutating state. Inventory reservations prevent the same rubbing from being collected, stolen, or abandoned twice during a transition.

### Hidden information

Keep enemy inventories, undiscovered loot, exact carrier positions, and final-island selection out of unauthorized state payloads. A minimap overlay is not a security boundary. Use explicit per-player views and test actual network messages. Colyseus state views are a candidate mechanism for this filtering. [6]

### Operational controls

Validate message schemas, cap message size and frequency, enforce session ownership, and use expiring reconnect credentials. A public guest can have only one active match seat per session. Guests may play private tests; stronger account controls can precede public competitive play.

Record input logs and authoritative event traces around disputes and errors, with a short documented retention period. Restrict administrative access and avoid logging authentication tokens. Treat match time and inventory as server data even if the browser’s JavaScript is modified. Minification is not an anti-cheat strategy.

# Objective ledger

The pure rubbing ledger of §4 with duel settlement (§11's theft and PvP gold), §12's recovery caches and §11's small match economy. `@pirate-sea-race/simulation/objectives` has the state, the operations and the invariant. `@pirate-sea-race/content/objectives` has the types, rubbing ids, timings, the gold and economy rules and the zod validators for source sites and assignments. It is plain integer data with no clocks and no `Math.random`. Time is the world tick (20 per second), supplied by the caller. Positions are integer sub-units, supplied by the caller. `MatchRoom` runs it since `rubbings-theft` (below, and `docs/architecture.md`).

## Content

- **Types:** `sun`, `moon`, `tide`, `storm`. This fixed order breaks ties. The slice plays the first two.
- **Rubbing ids:** `<type>-<copy>`, such as `tide-2`. The slice has `sun-1…4` and `moon-1…4`. The alpha has three copies of each of the four types.
- **Sources:** `assignSources(preset, sites, seed)` deals the rubbings onto the caller's sites with a seeded Fisher–Yates shuffle. There must be `sourcesPerIsland` sites per island. Source ids are `source-<island>-<slot>`. `sourceAssignmentSchema` checks the result: each rubbing sealed once, the right island counts, unique ids, and types that match the ids.
- **`objectiveRules`:**

  | Rule | Value |
  | --- | --- |
  | Collection | 120 ticks (6 s) |
  | Cache claim | 120 ticks (6 s) |
  | Theft choice window | 100 ticks (5 s) |
  | Repeated-win window | 3600 ticks (3 min) |
  | Seat reservation | 1200 ticks (60 s) |
  | Starting gold | 50 |
  | PvP gold | 20 %, capped at 50 |
  | Ruin gold, supply cache gold | 40, 25 |
  | Rumor price, tonic price | 25, 30 |
  | Tonic duration | 240 ticks (12 s) |
  | Tonic carry limit | 2 |

  `pvpGoldTransfer` rounds down and never takes more than the loser has. `grantAmount` and `itemPrice` read the economy's amounts. The validator refuses negative amounts, a zero duration and a carry limit below 1.

## State (`LedgerState`, all covered by `hashState`)

- `preset`, `rules`, `types`, `tick` (the latest tick an applied request carried), `rng`.
- `sources`: id, island, position, type, the rubbing sealed there, and `unlocked`. Sources start locked; the first solve of their island's ruin unlocks them (`ruins-online`).
- `rubbings`: id, type, `revision` (bumped on every move) and **one** `location`:
  - `source` (only its own source);
  - `player`;
  - `cache`;
  - `duel` (reserved for one of the two players of an unresolved duel).

  This is the only record of where a rubbing is. Inventories and cache contents are derived from it, so no second copy can disagree.
- `players`:
  - `gold`;
  - `status`: `connected`, `disconnected` (with `reservationDeadline`) or `left` (with `exit`, the position its leftover rubbings go to);
  - `channel`: a collection or cache claim, with `startTick`, `lastTick` and `heldTicks`;
  - `freshRumors`: the types lost to theft, a flag for the future rumor system;
  - `tonics` (0–2) and `tonicEffect` (the last activation's `startTick` and `endTick`, or null).
- `caches`: id, position and creation tick. A cache never despawns and is removed as soon as it is empty.
- `duels`: the players, `phase` (`fighting`, `choosing` or `settled`), `result`, `choiceDeadline` and `settlement`. A duel is committed once, and settled duels are kept so that a repeated message finds its result.
- `wins`: the last win tick per ordered (winner, loser) pair.
- `goldIssued`: the gold brought in by joining players (the rules' `startingGold` each; the caller never picks a purse) and minted by grants. Settlements only move gold.
- `goldSpent`, `supplyCaches` (the supply-cache ids given to `createLedger`), `grants` and `requests`: see Economy.

## Operations

Each operation is `(state, request) → { state, result }` and never writes into its input. `result.ok` tells whether it applied. `transfers` lists every move, with the before and after revisions (§16). `cacheIds` lists the caches opened.

| Operation | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| `addPlayer` | Joins with the rules' starting purse. An id is never reused. |
| `unlockSource` | Makes a source collectable. |
| `startCollect`, `startCacheClaim` | Start a hold at an unlocked, still-sealed source or on one selected cached rubbing, for a type the player lacks. Not while disconnected or in a duel. |
| `progressChannel` | One held tick. It must be exactly `lastTick + 1`: after a gap the hold was interrupted and counts from 0 again. At 120 ticks the target is checked again and the rubbing moves. If another player already took it, or the type is now held, the result is `failed` and nothing moves. |
| `cancelChannel` | Drops the hold and its progress. |
| `reserveDuel` | Reserves both players' rubbings in the duel and cancels their channels. A disconnected player can be challenged. |
| `reportDuelResult` | `win`, `draw` or `cancelled` (both players forfeited on the same tick). A draw or cancellation returns everything. A win with no eligible type settles at once and gold still moves. Otherwise it opens the choice window. |
| `chooseTheft` | The winner only, before `choiceDeadline`, for an eligible type (held by the loser, lacked by the winner). It commits. |
| `advanceLedger` | Call once per tick first. It settles every window whose deadline has come with the default theft: fewest sealed sources of the type, then type order. |
| `disconnectPlayer`, `reconnectPlayer` | Cancel the channel and reserve the seat. Returning before the deadline reattaches the same player. A repeated disconnect keeps the original deadline. |
| `expireReservation`, `quitPlayer` | Remove the player. Outside a duel, all their rubbings go into one cache at the given position (none if they hold nothing). In a fight, the player forfeits: the opponent wins, and what the winner does not take goes into a cache when the duel settles. A forfeit win never commits within its own tick; with nothing to steal, its window lasts one tick. So when both players leave on the same tick, whichever arrives first, the duel is `cancelled` (§12) and each inventory goes into its owner's cache. As the loser in a choice window, the same happens at settlement. As the winner in a choice window, the duel settles at once, and the winner takes neither rubbing nor gold. |

The commit checks eligibility again. It then moves the stolen rubbing and adds the lost type to the loser's `freshRumors`. Gold moves unless the same winner beat the same loser less than 3600 ticks before; theft still applies then. Last, the commit returns every other reserved rubbing to its owner, or to a cache if the owner has left. A disconnected loser therefore loses one rubbing first, and the rest is cached only at expiry, so nothing is duplicated.

## Idempotency (§16)

A request that is stale (its tick is before `state.tick`), duplicate, unauthorised, malformed or already resolved returns `ok: false`, a `rejection` reason, the current duel, channel or player, and **the very same state object**. The one exception is a purchase refused by a decision, which is kept so that its retry gets the same answer (see Economy). Examples:

- A second result message for a duel gets `already-resolved`, even if it names another winner.
- A choice after the commit gets `already-resolved`, and a late one gets `choice-window-closed`.
- A repeated start gets `duplicate` and keeps the progress.
- A second quit gets `player-left`.

There is no operation to drop, trade, copy or destroy a rubbing.

## Invariant

`checkLedger(state)` returns every violation, and `assertLedger` throws on any. It checks the following:

- Every rubbing id of the preset is present exactly once, with its type, and there are no others.
- Each location is valid: the rubbing's own source, a player still in the match who is not in a duel, an existing cache, or an unresolved duel that the named player is in.
- No player holds two rubbings of one type, counting reserved ones.
- No cache is empty.
- No player is in two unresolved duels, and nobody channels while disconnected, gone or in a duel.
- Gold is non-negative, and balances + `goldSpent` = `goldIssued`.
- `goldIssued` = players who ever joined × `startingGold` + the recorded grants, so gold minted outside the rules is caught even when the balances add up.
- Each grant key is one the ledger knows (an island's ruin, a listed supply cache), appears once and pays the rules' amount.
- (player, request id) is unique among `requests`, the purchases' prices sum to `goldSpent` (refusals cost nothing), and each player keeps at most 16 refusals, each a decided one.
- Each player carries 0–2 tonics and has one effect slot, so at most one tonic is active. A player who left has neither.
- The state is plain safe-integer data.

The tests run every operation on a deeply frozen state and check the invariant after each one. The property tests run 3 seeded matches per preset, 1200 random steps each, about 2700–5100 operations each. The steps cover:

- joins, collections, interruptions and contested completions;
- duels won, lost, drawn and cancelled;
- choices, wrong choices and timeouts;
- replayed messages;
- disconnects, returns, expiries, quits and cache claims;
- stale requests;
- from a second seeded stream between those steps, so the match around them is unchanged: ruin and supply grants (repeated keys included), purchases (request ids shared between players, retried purchases and refusals, and a player's id reused for the other item included) and tonic use.

After every step they also check the gold identity, non-negative gold and the tonic count directly, and that a tonic only activates when none was active for that player. A rejected step must return its input state, except a purchase refusal decided for the first time, which may only add itself to `requests`. The same seed replays to the same `hashState`.

## Economy (§11)

The economy is for route choices and recovery, so it only mints gold by rule and only spends it at the shop. Amounts come from `objectiveRules`; the caller never passes one.

- **`grantGold({ playerId, reason, sourceKey, tick })`** mints `ruin` gold (40) once per (player, ruin) and `supply-cache` gold (25) once per cache id for the whole match. `goldIssued` grows by the amount. Keys are bounded by what the ledger knows: a ruin's key is exactly `String(island)` for one of its sources' islands (the ruins module's key), a supply cache's is one of the `supplyCaches` given to `createLedger` (none given: no supply-cache grants). Any other key is `unknown-source`, so inventing keys mints nothing. The same player repeating a key gets `duplicate`; a cache someone else claimed gets `already-claimed`, with the earlier grant. A player who left gets nothing. The ruin and cache features decide when a grant is earned.
- **`purchase({ requestId, playerId, item, tick })`** buys a `rumor` (25) or a `tonic` (30). The gold goes to `goldSpent`, not to another player. Request ids are **per player**: outcomes are kept in `requests` under (player, request id), so another player's id never refuses or reveals yours. The refusals `insufficient-gold`, `tonic-limit` (a third tonic), `player-in-duel` (fighting or in the choice window) and `player-disconnected` are decisions: they are kept, charge nothing, and change only `requests` and `tick`. Malformed, stale, `unknown-player` and `player-left` requests are not kept and return the same state. Repeating a kept (player, request id) with the same item returns the original outcome, a purchase (`ok: true`) or a refusal (`ok: false`, with `refusal`), with `replayed: true` and the same state, whatever its tick and even if it would succeed now: a retry never pays twice and never buys what was refused. The same id for the other item is a `duplicate`. A player keeps at most 16 refusals; the 17th evicts their oldest, and an evicted id is decided afresh if retried. Purchases are never evicted.
- **`useTonic({ playerId, tick, atSea, grappled })`** spends one tonic and makes it active on ticks `tick` to `tick + 239`: `activeTonic(state, playerId, t)` is true from `startTick` and false from `endTick` = `startTick + 240`. It is refused with `tonic-active`, `no-tonic`, `not-at-sea`, `grappled` (a flag the caller sets from the world), `player-in-duel` or `player-disconnected`. A lost duel keeps the tonics; leaving drops them and ends an active one. The ledger never touches movement: the sailing code reads `activeTonic` and adds its +10 %.
- **The invariant** becomes balances + `goldSpent` = `goldIssued`, with `goldIssued` = purses + grants.
- **Rumor flow** (`intel-online`, `docs/architecture.md`): the ledger has no refunds, so the room charges only for a lead intel has delivered. A request whose (player, client request id) the room already decided is answered from the room's own record first (a bought id resends its lead, a refused id the same refusal; it keeps every bought id and each player's last 16 refusals). Otherwise the request waits for a world tick (a pending id's retry is merged, and a third waiting request is refused `busy`). Each tick the room takes at most one request per player, in the reveal step, after every other ledger operation of the tick. It checks, in order, that the buyer's ship is moored at a dock on this tick, the ledger's state (connected, out of any duel, at least the rumor's price, read with `findPlayer`, `activeDuelOf` and `itemPrice`) and intel's `targetedRumorAvailability`; a failure is a refusal the room records, and the ledger never sees it. Then it runs `stepIntel` with the request under a room-namespaced id (`<player>:<client id>`), and only when the outcome is `rumor` or `sighting` does it call `purchase` with the client's id. Nothing moves that player's gold between the check and the charge, so a refused `purchase` there is a broken invariant: the world tick throws and the match faults (§12). Every other outcome is one recorded refusal with intel's reason, uncharged. The ledger's own replay of a purchase id is therefore never reached by the room. `economy-online`'s dockside shop will list the same purchase.

## In the match room (`rubbings-theft`)

- **One ledger per match**, the `slice` preset, seeded with the ledger's own seed derived from the match's private master secret (`deriveSeed(secret, "ledger")`, `match-online`; never the public match code, and never a seed another system reads). Its sites are each regular island's docks, `sourcesPerIsland` (2) per island in dock id order, reusing a dock when an island has fewer (`apps/server/src/objectives.ts`). **Every source is locked at match start** behind its island's ruin (`ruins-online`): the ruin's first solve calls `unlockSource` for each of the island's sources, and every solver's completion calls `grantGold` (reason `ruin`, key `String(island)`). Only the test-only ruins setting opens islands from the start.
- **Holding E** moored at a dock of island *i* works on its first eligible target: the island's sealed, unlocked source of a type the player lacks, else a rubbing of a type they lack in a recovery cache at that dock, each in the fixed type order. The room calls `startCollect`/`startCacheClaim`, then `progressChannel` every held tick, and `cancelChannel` when E is released, the ship casts off or the target is gone. A reservation (`reserveDuel`) and a disconnect cancel it inside the ledger.
- **Duels** follow `docs/encounters.md`'s mapping; the room reports `settled` to the rules only once the ledger has settled.
- **§12** runs on the world tick: `disconnectPlayer` at a drop, `reconnectPlayer` on return, `expireReservation` at the deadline (the cache at the dock the rules would respawn the player at), `quitPlayer` for every other leave (the cache at the dock nearest the ship by sea).
- **The room asserts `checkLedger`** after every world tick in development and tests, together with its own invariant (no ship removed while the ledger counts its player in the match).
- **Test-only settings** (never read by the production entry): `startGameServer({ matchTuning })` overrides the rules (for example a short seat reservation) and pins the source layout; the dev entry reads `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_SOURCES` (and `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_RUINS`, which opens islands from the start).
- **The results** (`match-online`): the room reads each tick's accomplishments from the ledger alone, by diffing where each rubbing is (from a source: collected; from a cache: a cache claimed; from one player to another: a theft and a loss; from a player into a cache: a loss), each balance, and the islands players moor at, and reports them to the match flow with each settled duel. Holding every type (`holdsAllTypes`) is the claim's eligibility.

## Decisions taken here

- The slice uses Sun and Moon.
- "Free sources" in the default theft counts sealed sources, locked or not. Cached rubbings are not counted.
- The repeated-win window is measured from the previous win over that opponent, whether or not that win paid gold.
- A winner who leaves during the choice window takes nothing.
- Both players leaving a fight on the same tick cancel it, in either order. The ledger cannot rely on processing order (§7), so a forfeit waits one tick before it can commit.
- Both players stay reserved until the choice window settles, so neither can collect or be challenged during it.
- Economy grants and purchases check whether the player has left. Grants ignore duels and disconnects, because the reward was earned before. Purchases and tonics refuse disconnected players, as channels do.
- A repeated purchase id is looked up before the tick check, so a retry still finds its purchase or refusal after the clock moved on.
- Kept refusals are bounded (16 per player, oldest evicted) so a client cannot grow the state without limit; purchases are bounded by gold.
- Every purse is the rules' `startingGold`; tests that need other purses change that rule for the whole ledger.

## The sea

- **Map format and pipeline.** The map is `assets/source/maps/slice-sea.tmj`, in a documented subset of Tiled's JSON format (`packages/content/src/tiledMap.ts`): orthogonal, finite, 24 px tiles (`TILE_PIXELS`, as drawn); one imageless tileset whose tiles carry a `type` of `sea`, `land`, `shoal` or `dock`; a `terrain` layer as a plain gid array; an `objects` layer with a named `island` rectangle per island (property `theme`), a `spawn` point per slot (properties `slot` and `facing`), one `hub` point and a `final-socket` rectangle per hidden final-island socket (properties `claimX`, `claimY`). Nothing else is accepted. `npm run map:build -- --from-sketch` writes it from one mirrored ASCII quarter (`sliceSeaSketch.ts`; a unit test fails when they differ). `npm run map:build` (`scripts/build-map.ts`) converts it into `packages/content/src/maps/sliceSea.ts` (terrain rows and objects) if it passes the validators; a unit test fails when that module is stale (it reads the `.tmj` as a Vite `?raw` import).
- **Validators.** `validateSeaMap` checks §2 and §3: four small islands, each land tile inside one island rectangle, each island with a dock beside its land and open sea; shoals; eight spawn slots on open sea outside the islands' bounding box and pairwise beyond `ENGAGEMENT_RANGE_TILES` (6); every dock and slot reachable by sea; and, by eight-way shortest paths (no cut corners) at full speed: each island's nearest neighbour 20–40 s away, no dock-or-slot crossing over 90 s, a dock within 40 s of every slot. No empty stretches: every navigable tile is within the sight radius of land, a shoal or a dock (not the edge). Routes meet: closing the disc around the hub (open sea near the centre; radius half the sight radius, so ships in it see each other) lengthens or cuts every shortest path between docks of different islands. At most 112 tiles a side. The slice sea (111 × 111 tiles, midline shoal walls open only in the hub's disc): neighbours 26 s apart, longest crossing 39 s, water within 9.9 tiles of an obstacle, 90 % open sea.
- **Final-island sockets (§3, §5).** `map.finalSockets` holds four identical dockless islets (one sketched islet, mirrored), each a rectangle of at most 6 × 6 and a claim tile: open sea beside it whose centre is the claim point, the treasure circle's centre (`TREASURE_CIRCLE_RADIUS_TILES`). Their land belongs to no island, so docks, sailing, respawn and visibility treat them as plain land. `validateFinalSockets` (run by `validateSeaMap`) requires every claim tile within 90 s of every spawn slot, at least two sockets in any sector holding one (`sectorOf`: column ⌊3x / width⌋, row ⌊3y / height⌋, the grid a sector reveal must use), at least 12 navigable tiles in each circle, and nothing within 8 tiles of what the running tests sail on (`keepClearZones`: the hub, spawn slots, docks and 14 tiles out from each, the out-of-sight row). The slice's sockets sit two in the north-centre sector and two in the south-centre one. `chooseFinalSocket(map, secret)` picks a match's socket from a 32-bit secret, mixed by the murmur3 finaliser before the modulo; the room passes the final-socket seed derived from its master secret (below), never the public match seed (§3).
- **Sailing.** Positions are integer sub-units (256 a tile); headings are 64 directions, 0 east, clockwise (y grows down), unit vectors from a literal cosine table. Each world step the throttle (`-moveY`) accelerates towards 39 sub-units a step (3.05 tiles a second), brakes, or reverses slowly from a stop; `moveX` turns a heading a step (3.2 s a circle); none coasts to a stop. On 24 px tiles that handles like the old five tiles a second on 16 px (unit tests hold it within 25 %). The hull is a square of half-size 80. A move sweeps each axis in turn through the tile columns or rows its leading edge enters and stops flush at the first land, shoal or map edge: a ship slides along an obstacle met at an angle, runs aground head-on, and never tunnels.
- **Docking.** Hold E near a dock to moor, at any speed and throttle: with the hull on or beside the berth tile (unless pinched), or within reach with a clear run, it moves to the dock's centre, stops and records the dock, so no arrival slides past or grounds beside it (pinched: a diagonal tile whose two shared tiles are blocked). It stays moored while E or the throttle it moored with (`mooredThrottle`) is held; pressing a throttle after releasing that one, or the other direction, casts off. An input stall does not: the server gives a lapsed client no input, keeping the latch. The keyboard keeps a tap of E until its next read (not past a blur), the server until its next step; then the HUD says "Press E to dock".
- **Visibility boundary (§16).** The room's `ships` map is tagged `.view()`. Each client gets a `StateView`; after every mirror the room puts into it the client's own ship and each ship within `SIGHT_RADIUS_TILES` (10, `visibleShipIds`) and takes out the rest, so Colyseus encodes no other ship into that client's patches (an integration test checks the decoded state and the raw frames). `players` in the state is the public player count. Terrain is static content every client bundles, never synced; the minimap fog is client-side presentation, not a security boundary.
- **Test placement.** Tests place ships through `POST /__test/place` (code, session id, tile, heading); its tile bounds come from the map. Only the dev and test entry (`apps/server/src/dev.ts`) offers it, when `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_PLACEMENT=1`; the production entry (`main.ts`) never reads that, which the build smoke test checks. It only moves a ship: admission and views are unchanged.
- **Test-only timings and source layout.** `startGameServer({ matchTuning })` gives every match of that server other rules (a short seat reservation, respawn wait, duel absence, ready countdown, protection, match, channel or reveals), a pinned source layout, a pinned final-socket secret, an injected master secret, or the room's test hooks; integration tests use it and assert in world ticks. Colyseus has one matchmaker per process, so each integration test file has one set of timings. The dev and test entry also reads `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_SOURCES` (for example `sun,sun|moon,moon|sun,sun|moon,moon`: the types of each island's sources), `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_TIMINGS` (for example `ready=60,protection=20,match=12000`, in world ticks; also `sectors`, `carriers` and `carrierInterval`, which sets the sighting delay with it) and `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_FINAL_SECRET` (a 32-bit secret), which the browser tests set so a scenario picks an island by type, plays a whole match in a minute and knows where the treasure is. With the test placement setting, `POST /__test/next-match-timings` (the same timing names, as JSON) gives only the next match created its own timings, so the `match-deadline` scenario runs a 20 s match. All are off by default. `main.ts` reads none (the build smoke test starts it with all of them set, malformed, and checks the 20 s countdown and 35:00 timer) and turns the per-tick invariant checks off.

## Duels at sea (§6, §14)

- **Encounters in the room.** Each world step (20 Hz), after sailing, `MatchRoom` builds one tick's snapshot for `stepEncounters` (`docs/encounters.md`): every ship's position and heading, whether its player is connected, pressed J since the last world step (the input gate latches it like E), has a loaded arena, or started a collection or cache claim last tick (which ends protection). It passes back the reports of duels that ended since the last tick, and a settlement only once the ledger has settled the duel (below). It applies the events: `duel-reserved` stops both ships and opens a duel; `duel-started` starts its fight; a ready-check `duel-forfeited` or `duel-cancelled` ends it; `player-returned` and `player-respawned` move a ship (at rest, or moored at the dock). Everything else (attempts, protection, cooldowns, the loser's wait) is mirrored from the rules' state. The rules alone choose targets and decide hits, returns and docks; the room never re-implements them. Reserved and respawning ships get neutral input, so they stay put. The rules run from control start, when every ship gets the departure's protection, to the match's end.
- **One duel machine.** `DuelMachine` (`apps/server/src/duelMachine.ts`) is the duel both rooms run: seats and their `DuelInputGate`s, the 180-frame countdown with round-trip probes and the input delay chosen 30 frames before its end, `DuelAuthority` stepped at 60 Hz with a bundle every third frame, the reconnection bookkeeping (a dropped seat is marked away, a returning one gets a snapshot bundle) and the forfeits decided together at the next frame boundary. It writes a lobby schema and sends through a callback, so the host room owns clients and timers. `DuelRoom` starts the fight when the countdown ends (`start: "countdown"`); the match room starts it when the rules say so (`start: "called"`: the countdown is over and both fighters sent `duel-ready`). A countdown that ends with a fighter still loading waits in `waiting`, without probes; clients drop their session then, so the fight's clock starts from the fight's own frames. The match room keeps each duel's lobby for 2 s after settlement (`DUEL_RESULT_TICKS`), so both read the result.
- **Absence** (§12, on the world tick). A drop anywhere disconnects the player in the ledger at once; the ship stays in the world, grapplable, for the 60 s seat reservation. A fighter away from an unfinished duel for 10 s, counted in world ticks, forfeits it (both on one frame: no winner, reported as cancelled) and respawns as any loser, keeping the seat. Back before the deadline, the same player continues (`reconnectPlayer`; a fighter gets a snapshot bundle). Colyseus' `allowReconnection` is only an outer bound (70 s) that keeps the socket's slot: at the ledger's deadline the room expires the reservation and releases that slot; any other leave (consented, or the outer bound first) is a quit.
- **Message routing.** A duel's `duel-frames` and `duel-probe` go to its two fighters only, with `client.send`; the room routes `duel-input`, `duel-probe` echoes and `duel-ready` from a session to that session's duel and drops them from anyone else. In the browser, `SeaDuelRouter` listens for the duel messages once per match and hands them to the duel client of the moment (a message that beats the lobby's patch waits for it); the same `DuelClient` runs a standalone duel over `duelRoomTransport`.
- **Visibility (§16).** The state's new maps are filtered per client like `ships`: `grapples` (telegraphs, keyed by attacker: in a view when its attacker is in sight, or when it is the viewer's own or aimed at the viewer; each attempt has a twin entry with `defenderId` blank, which a viewer who sees the attacker but not the target gets instead), `markers` (a duel's position and reservation tick under an opaque key, nothing naming its fighters: in a view within the sight radius), `caches` (a recovery cache's position and dock, no contents: within the sight radius), `status` (a player's own mode, protection, cooldown, respawn tick, gold, rubbings, holds and theft choice: only the player's), `duels` (a duel's lobby: only its two fighters'). A ship in a duel or waiting to respawn is in no one else's view; its own player keeps it until it waits to respawn. A fighter or a player waiting to respawn sees no other ship, telegraph, marker or cache. Integration tests check the decoded state and the raw frames of a watcher in sight and a stranger out of it.
- **One game per page.** The match page's Phaser game (canvas renderer, see Rendering) holds the sea scene and the arena scene (`MatchGame`); a duel puts the sea to sleep and starts the arena with that duel's client, and its end wakes the sea (`data-scene` says which shows). The duel HUD moves beside the canvas meanwhile. The standalone duel page's game holds only the arena. The helm is read during a duel (so a J tapped in the arena is not kept) but the sea gets neutral input.

## Rubbings in the room (§4, §11, §12, `rubbings-theft`)

- **The ledger per match.** `MatchRoom` creates a `slice` ledger with the match, seeded with the ledger's seed derived from the match's master secret (below). Its sources stand at each regular island's docks (two per island, in dock id order), all locked at the start behind their island's ruin (below). The ledger is the only writer of rubbings and gold: the room calls its operations and never moves either itself, and after every world tick it checks `checkLedger` and its own invariant (every player the ledger counts in the match has a ship, and every ship such a player) in development and tests.
- **Collecting and claiming.** Holding E moored at a dock is one gesture: E moors, and the same hold works on E's target there, the island's first sealed source of a type the player lacks, else a cached rubbing of a type they lack (fixed type order). The room continues the ledger's hold every held tick; releasing E, casting off, a reservation or a disconnect cancels it, and progress resets. Starting a hold ends protection on the next tick.
- **Settlement order.** Each world tick: `advanceLedger` (a closed choice window settles with the default); the §12 timers (duel absence, seat expiry into a cache at the respawn dock); `stepEncounters`, whose reports tell the ledger a fight's result and the rules its end, and tell the rules `settled` only once the ledger has settled the duel; then the holds. On a win with an eligible type the winner gets 5 s to send `choose-theft`; a draw, a cancellation or a win with nothing to take settles at once (gold still moves on a win). Only then do the players return. The fighters' lobby stays in their views through the choice. Both fighters' notices carry the settlement's tick, who picked the stolen type (`settledChoice`: the winner, or the default at the deadline) and the choice's deadline tick, so the purse says "You chose Moon" (the loser: "They chose Moon") or "Taken by default: Moon". The web client maps a key (1, 2) or a click on option *n* to a type in one pure function (`chosenTheftType`, `hud.ts`) before it sends `choose-theft`.
- **Messages.** `choose-theft` `{ duel, type }` (protocol v6): the opaque key of the sender's own duel lobby and a rubbing type, zod-validated, at most 64 bytes and 8 a second; the ledger accepts it only from the winner, inside the window, for an eligible type, and otherwise changes nothing. Collecting and claiming need no message: E is the input's interact button.
- **What each client sees (§16).** Gold, rubbings, the hold and its progress, the last results and the theft options are in the player's own `status` entry only. The island's sealed types and a cache's contents are filled in only while the player is moored at that island's dock or that cache's dock. A cache is a contentless marker at its dock, in a view within the sight radius. Integration tests check raw frames: an onlooker in sight of a collection and a duel never receives a rubbing type at all.
- **Leaving.** A reservation that expires removes the player and puts their remaining rubbings in one cache at the dock the encounter rules would respawn them at; a quit (or Colyseus' outer bound) forfeits a duel in progress and caches the rest at the dock nearest the ship by sea (`seaPathCosts`). No path removes a ship while the ledger still counts its player. A cache never despawns; claimed empty, it disappears.

## The match: lobby to results (§2, §5, `match-online`)

- **Lobby.** A private match opens in its lobby (`phase` in the synced state). Its players are listed by seat ("Player 2": join order from 1; the state names players by seat, never by session id, so a ship's id still first reaches a client when it comes into sight, §16). The first joiner hosts (the host passes on when they leave). The host's `start-match` (zod-validated, empty, 4 a second) goes to the match module's `stepMatch`, which starts the ready countdown with 2 or more players present (`PRIVATE_MIN_PLAYERS`, the single source: the content's `privateMatch.minPlayers` of 1 is stale and not read) and otherwise answers `start-refused` (`too-few`, `not-host`). From the countdown on, the listing's metadata says `started` and admission refuses every join with "That match has already started" (the room's `onJoin` refuses too); if the countdown falls back to the lobby (fewer than 2 left), joins reopen. Ships are held (neutral input) until control starts and again after the end.
- **Ready and departure.** The countdown lasts `readyTicks` (20 s) while clients preload (the arena scene is already in the page's one game). The flow's `control-started` tick starts everything that counts from it: the encounter rules' state with every ship protected for `spawnProtectionTicks` (45 s, which is also the departure's length), the claim config (`createClaimConfig`, its `deadlineTick` = control start + `deadlineTicks`), and the intel config and state. The timer the HUD shows is the match module's `remaining`, whose duration is the claim rules' `deadlineTicks`: both configs are built from one set of rules (`matchConfigFor`, `claimConfigFor` in `matchFlow.ts`), and a unit test and an integration test hold them equal.
- **Tick order** (each world tick while the match runs): `advanceLedger` (closed theft windows), the §12 timers, then §5's order: **encounters** (accepted reservations first) → the ledger's holds → **claim** channel (`stepClaim`: every ship with `holdsAllTypes` from the ledger, E, speed, connection, protection, and the encounter rules' reserved duels) → **match flow** (`stepMatch`: the joins, leaves and starts since the last tick, this tick's accomplishments, and the claim's `match-ended`) → **intel reveals** (`stepIntel`). Before control start only the timers and the flow run. The room drops reports naming a player the flow does not know before calling `stepMatch`, which throws on them: one pure function over plain data (`knownFlowReports`, `apps/server/src/flowReports.ts`) drops a duel's end naming an unknown fighter, a claim's end naming an unknown winner and any accomplishment naming an unknown player, and keeps joins (known from then on), leaves, starts and a fault. The room handles no `join-refused`: it reports a join only in the lobby (its `onJoin` refuses one after it), a lobby step applies joins before any start, and the room seats no more players than the flow has seats.
- **Reveals.** The intel module reads the ledger's rubbings and the ships each tick; the room delivers its reveal messages (rumors and the journal: "Intel in the room" below), each to exactly its recipients as `reveal` messages: `final-sector` to everyone at the first full set (also kept in the public state, name and index only), `final-route` with the exact claim point only to a full-set holder (also in that player's own status, with `routeActive`), `final-route-inactive` to a holder who lost a type, `sector-reveal` at minute 20 and `carrier-report` from minute 28 to everyone. An integration test checks raw frames: the other player never receives a `final-route`. The browser shows each as a banner and in a list; the holder gets a compass line, an arrow off the ship, the treasure circle and a minimap mark. In play everyone else's compass shows, inactive, "No treasure route yet". The page's decisions are pure view models, unit-tested branch by branch: `flowView` (`flowHud.ts`: each flow element's text, state and visibility) and `treasureView` (`treasureView.ts`: the sea scene's circle, marks, arrow and claim rings as shapes); `applyFlow` and the scene's `drawTreasure` only copy them into the page and the canvas.
- **The claim.** Stopped inside the treasure circle, holding every type and E, unreserved: the claim module's channel runs for `channelTicks` (10 s); starting it ends protection (the encounter rules hear it on the next tick). The player's status carries the channel's progress and the last reset's reason (moved, released, a duel, a lost type, a disconnect); the claim panel shows them with "Any rival in range can interrupt you with a grapple". The room can't produce a lost-type reset (`ineligible`) in a running channel: a player loses a type only through a duel's theft, and that duel's reservation resets the channel (`duel`) on the tick it's accepted, long before the settlement moves the rubbing. The claim module's own tests cover that branch, and the claim panel's unit test shows its text. Ships channelling show it to whoever sees them (a gold ring), and those players are warned. On `match-ended` the flow ends the match: outcomes freeze (a running duel is cancelled without theft, no ship takes input, nothing more is stepped).
- **Endings and results.** `claim` (a winner), `deadline` (no winner; a claim completing on the deadline tick wins), `abandoned` (everyone left after the lobby; stepped once more as the room closes) and `aborted` (a world tick that throws is caught as §12's server fault). The accomplishments come from the ledger (`docs/objectives.md`) and settled duels: a rubbing leaving a source is collected, one leaving a cache is a cache claimed, one moving between players is a theft and a loss, one going into a cache is a loss; a settled win or draw counts for both fighters, a cancelled duel for neither. The results (the flow's) are synced to everyone once, shown until players leave (Back to lobby returns to the landing page), and recorded in the process's outcome list (`recordedOutcomes`).
- **Seeds.** One private master secret per match (32 bytes of `node:crypto`'s `randomBytes`). Each system's seed is `HMAC-SHA-256(secret, "pirate-sea-race/seed/v1/" + system)`, its first four bytes: `world`, `ledger`, `final-socket`, `intel`, `ruins`, `fruit` (the fruit deal), and each duel's `duel:<n>`. No raw seed feeds two systems and none reads the match code; unit and integration tests check both.
- **Test hooks** (`MatchTuning.testHooks`, off by default, never set by an entry point): `injectFault()` makes the next world tick throw (an `aborted` ending), `pauseAfterTick(tick)` / `resumeWorld()` stop the clock after a tick so a test lines an input up with an exact tick (a grapple landing on a claim's completion tick, a claim completing on the deadline tick), `injectFlowReports(reports)` adds reports to the flow's next step (a ghost player's, to show they are dropped), and `testBuyRumor(id, request)` with `stepWorldTicks(n)` lines targeted rumor requests up with exact ticks while the clock is paused.
- **Coverage.** `docs/match-online-coverage.md` lists every room-level and UI branch this feature added or changed, each with the test that fails without it. `recordedOutcomes` keeps only the latest 64 (`outcomes.ts`).

## Ruins in the room (§3, §11, §16, `ruins-online`)

- **Dealing.** `createMatchRuins` (`apps/server/src/ruins.ts`) deals `docs/ruins.md`'s ruins once per match from the `ruins` seed (never the ledger's, so no clue reveals the deal). Sources start locked. The first solve's `sources-unlocked` calls `unlockSource` for each of the island's sources. Every solver's `gold-awarded` calls `grantGold` (reason `ruin`, key the island), which pays 40 once per player per ruin.
- **Presence and messages** (protocol v8). Each world tick in play, after the encounters, the room reports every player's presence to the rules: the island they're moored at (if connected and in the world) and whether a duel holds them. A leave reports at once. So casting off, a duel or a disconnect closes the session by the next tick. `open-ruin` (R) sends `ruin-clue` back to the sender only, strictly parsed. `answer-ruin` `{ requestId, attempt }` is judged once per request id (the last 16 are remembered, and a retry gets the first verdict). `ruin-result` carries only the outcome, the refusal, the lockout tick, the gold paid and whether this solve unlocked the island. Both are zod-validated, 8 a second, and read only in play.
- **Visibility.** The player's own `status` carries the ruin's state where they're moored, their session and their lockout. The `ruins` map's per-island markers (`unlocked`) are in a view only when the island is within sight or the player is moored there. The answer never leaves the server; an integration test checks raw frames.
- **Browser.** `ruinPanel.ts` holds pure view models and entry logic, plus the `RuinPanel` controller that `main.ts` feeds. While it's open, the helm is neutral.
- **Test-only** `testRuins` (env `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_RUINS`, or the next-match route): `open` unlocks an island at the start. `sequence` or `navigation` pins its kind, using the first derived `ruins:<n>` seed that deals it. Integration tuning and the browser server open every island unless a test asks otherwise. The branch table is `docs/ruins-online-coverage.md`.

## Fruits in the room (§9, §16, `fruit-kits-online`)

- **Pedestals and the deal.** `pedestalSites` (`apps/server/src/fruits.ts`) puts one pedestal on each regular island at its highest-id dock (4 in the slice). `createMatchFruits` deals `docs/fruits.md`'s fruit onto them from the `fruit` seed. Test-only `testFruits` (env `PIRATE_SEA_RACE_TEST_FRUITS`, for example `ember,smoke,smoke,ember`) pins the kit on each island's pedestal. The browser server pins that deal.
- **Eating** (protocol v9). F held is the input's fruit button (`Buttons.Fruit`). Each world tick in play, after the encounters and the ruins' presence and before the ledger's holds and the claim, the room calls `stepFruits` with every player's facts: the pedestal at the dock they're moored at (none while waiting to respawn), F, whether a duel reserves them, and whether they're disconnected (a drop cancels the channel as `disconnected`). Each player's last result (`eaten:<kit>`, `interrupted:<reason>`, `refused:<reason>`) and progress go into their own `status` with their `kit`. A refusal at the pedestal they just emptied doesn't replace the meal.
- **Inspecting.** `inspect-fruit` (`{}`, 8 a second, in play, not in a duel) sends `fruit-kit` to the sender only: the island, and the kit with its §9 description, or `full: false`. Both are zod-validated and strict.
- **Visibility.** `pedestals` markers (island, dock, place, `full`) are in a view when the dock is in sight. The deal never leaves the server except in an inspection. A player's kit is only in their own status until a duel. At a duel's start the lobby's `kits` (`"ember,duelist"`, the attacker's first) reaches its two fighters. Integration tests check raw frames.
- **Into duels.** `openSeaDuel` passes `kitOf` for both fighters to the `DuelMachine`, which creates the frame-0 state with them and syncs them in the lobby. Both clients build the same state from the seed and the kits, so the per-frame hashes cover them. No rule clears a meal, so the kit survives a lost duel.
- **Browser.** `fruitPanel.ts` holds the HUD's kit and pedestal lines, the panel's view model and the `FruitPanel` controller. `pageKeys.ts` routes a keydown to one panel at a time and skips keys typed into text fields. `arenaDrawing.ts` turns `fighterTells` into the arena's shapes: bolts, smoke screens, the obscured startup flash, the reappearance, and threats only for striking moves. The arena reports `data-bolts`, `data-zones` and `data-tells`. The branch table is `docs/fruit-kits-online-coverage.md`.

## Intel in the room (§3, §11, §13, §16, `intel-online`)

- **Delivery.** Every intel message reaches its recipients (`intelMessageOf`, `apps/server/src/intel.ts`, copying fields by name): reveals as `reveal`, every lead (`rumor`, `carrier-sighting`, `no-lead`, `rumor-stale`, `rumor-resolved`) as `journal` to its one player (protocol v10). None names a player, a rubbing or a source's, cache's or ship's position.
- **Journal log.** Intel's journal keeps no sightings or links, so the room logs every lead each player receives (`JournalLog`, last 24; a stale entry links to the next lead of its type). The player's own `status` carries it (`journal`), with `knownIslands` (in sight by the ruin markers' rule or moored at, unlocked as last seen, solved by them) and `rumorOffers`, so a reconnecting player reads it back.
- **Targeted rumors** (`buy-rumor`). A decided (player, id) is answered from the room's record first (`RumorBook`: every purchase, the last 16 refusals); otherwise it waits (a pending id merges; a third is `busy`). Last in the world tick, at most one per player: refused unless moored at a dock now, connected, out of any duel, holding the price, and offered a lead by `targetedRumorAvailability`; then `stepIntel` under `<player>:<id>`, and `purchase` only for a delivered lead (a refusal there throws: the match faults). Anything else is one recorded, uncharged refusal.
- **Browser** (`journal.ts`). M opens the journal (Escape closes it; Tab moves among its buttons; the helm stays live): leads newest first with reasons, stale ones greyed with their replacement, known islands, types, kit, and a Buy button per missing type. A toast announces each new lead. Sector borders show only while the journal is open or a sector lead is active. Branch table: `docs/intel-online-coverage.md`.

## The duel (combat spike)

- **Duel room.** `DuelRoom` (room name `duel`) holds one duel, a `DuelMachine`: two players, or one in practice against a server-side dummy (player two, `idle` or `guard`). It is admitted like a match, by the same code in `admission.ts`: a one-time create token, `onAuth` before any seat, the room's own check, `filterBy(['code', 'createToken'])`, no token in the listing. The countdown (180 frames) starts once every seat is taken; the machine probes round trips during it, chooses the shared input delay 30 frames before the end, then steps `DuelAuthority` at 60 Hz, which the room's `setFixedTimestep` drives. Inputs go through `DuelInputGate`, which admits them through the same `InputAdmission` as the world's `InputGate` (the rate window and the protocol validation: 128 bytes, 60 a second of room ticks, increasing sequence, 30 frames of lead), so the two cannot drift, and counts its drops. Frame bundles and probes are Colyseus messages; the lobby is a small Schema state. A drop keeps the seat for 10 s (`allowReconnection`); leaving, or staying away, forfeits, decided at the next frame boundary (two forfeits on one frame: no winner). The rules and rates are in `docs/duel.md`.
- **One rollback core.** The browser's `DuelClient` and `HeadlessDuelClient` both run `DuelClientSession` from `@pirate-sea-race/simulation/netcode`; they differ only in transport and input (keyboard or script), and each plays over a duel room or a match room. The arena draws the predicted state; the HUD shows the confirmed one (server-equal health, stamina and clock) and the room's result. `window.__duel` exposes the session's counters and tagged inputs, read-only, for the browser tests.
- **Impairment harness.** `ImpairmentProxy` is a TCP proxy (Node's `net`, no new dependency) between a client and the server. It forwards HTTP untouched in content and splits the WebSocket stream into frames; each frame waits half the round trip plus a uniform ±jitter/2 in its direction, never overtaking the frame before it, and a lost frame (seeded draw) waits a further retransmission timeout (200 ms), stalling everything behind it as TCP does. `sever()` cuts every connection (optionally refusing new ones) for the reconnect tests. `tests/integration/duelHarness.test.ts` runs a scripted duel between two headless clients through it, with no impairment and at 80 ms, 20 ms jitter and 1 % loss (and `seaDuelHarness.test.ts` the same duel at sea, at 80 ms, 20 ms and 1 %, with the scripts and budget of `harness.ts`), and requires no desync at any of the ~180 hash checks per client, no rate-limited input, and corrections at most one per change of the opponent's applied input plus one per own substituted input, and at most 8 a second; it prints and annotates the counts. `replayRecordedDuel` replays a log (for example the pairs a client received) with `replayDuel`.
- **Dev latency.** Only in `npm run dev` (`import.meta.env.DEV`), `?rtt=80&jitter=20` on the page delays that browser's duel socket both ways, probes and pings included; the build smoke test checks the production bundle does not contain it. The browser checks run the production build, so the setting is tested below the browser: `apps/web/src/devLatency.ts` has no DOM dependency, unit tests with fake timers check its delay, jitter bound and order on a real SDK room, and `tests/integration/devLatency.test.ts` wraps two headless duel clients in it and checks that the room's measured round trip and chosen input delay rise above the same duel without it.

## Rules the code keeps

- **Purity.** `simulation`, `protocol` and `content` import no Phaser, Colyseus, DOM, timers, wall-clock time or `Math.random`, and call no floating-point transcendental `Math` function. Their tsconfig leaves out the DOM library, and `tests/unit/purity.test.ts` fails on a violation.
- **Integers.** Every number in the simulation state is an integer, so `hashState` (64-bit FNV-1a over a canonical serialization) does not depend on the JavaScript engine; a determinism test replays an input log.
- **One clock.** The room steps the simulation 60 times a second with Colyseus `setFixedTimestep`. World logic runs on frames divisible by 3 (20 Hz); the room then mirrors the plain state into its Schema and updates the views, and Colyseus patches clients at 20 Hz. Integration tests measure both rates, also with eight sailing ships in one cluster.
- **The server alone writes state.** At sea, clients send only `input` messages, `{seq, frame, buttons, moveX, moveY}`, validated with zod (the grapple is a button: the rules choose its target, never the client). `InputGate` drops, and counts, messages over the rate cap (60 per second of match time) or over 128 bytes, malformed ones (axes outside [-1, 1] are rejected, not clamped), ones whose sequence number is not higher than the last, and ones tagged more than 30 frames ahead. A held input lapses to neutral after 15 frames. An accepted world input applies from the server's next step whatever frame it names, since sea clients do not predict; a duel input applies to exactly the frame it names (`docs/duel.md`). Unknown message types are dropped. Colyseus's own limits sit above these: WebSocket frames over 4 KiB and more than 240 messages a second disconnect.
- **Joining.** Matches and duels share the admission code (`apps/server/src/admission.ts`). Colyseus serves `create`, `join`, `joinOrCreate` and `joinById` over HTTP and reserves a seat before any room check, so a private match is guarded in three layers. The static `onAuth` runs before any seat is reserved: it refuses a mismatched protocol version (426), a request with neither or both of `code` and `createToken` or a malformed token (400), an unknown code (404) and a full match (409). A `create` carries a one-time 128-bit `createToken`; the room is registered with `filterBy(['code', 'createToken'])` and `onCreate` replaces the listing's metadata with `{code, custom}`, so no listing holds a token and a token-carrying request can only create. The room's `onJoin` is the backstop, and the only check `joinById` meets after `onAuth`: one seat with the creator's token, then only seats with the match's code (404 for another code, 403 otherwise). Refusal messages stay under 100 bytes. A caller who knows a room id can still hold seats with `joinById` until each reservation expires (15 s).
- **Rendering.** The client draws the ships the server sent 100 ms in the past, interpolating between patches; the HUD lists their latest decoded state. The camera follows the player's ship over a Phaser tile map at 24 px a tile (the 480 px sight circle fits the 800 × 560 view) that leaves open sea to the background colour, keeping a software renderer fast. The sea does not predict; the duel arena does (above). Both of the page's Phaser games use the canvas renderer: the standalone duel page's arena, and the match page's one game holding the sea and the arena. With `Phaser.AUTO`, headless Chromium (the GPU-less case: WebGL in software, on the main thread) starved the duel client's 4 ms tick during a fight at sea: the `sea-duel` browser test, which records the `DuelClient`'s tick intervals through the fight and requires their 95th percentile under 16 ms, measured 135 ms. With `Phaser.CANVAS` it measured 8.5 ms (host) and 8.9 ms (guest), so the match game now uses the canvas renderer too.

## Toolchain

npm workspaces, TypeScript 7.0.2 (`tsc` for typechecks and package builds), Vite 8.3.0 (web build), Vitest 5.0.1 (`unit` and `integration` projects), tsx (map script and dev server), Phaser 4.2.1, `colyseus` 0.18.8 with `@colyseus/schema` 5.0.34, `@colyseus/sdk` 0.18.4, zod 4.6.5, Playwright 1.63.0. Workspace packages export a `@pirate-sea-race/source` condition that points at `src/`, so development and tests need no build. `npm run build` compiles each package to `dist/` in dependency order, then `scripts/smoke-build.ts` loads every workspace export from `dist/`, starts the built server on a free port, creates a match through the SDK, waits for the creator's ship and checks that no test-placement route answers, with every wait bounded. `colyseus` brings `uWebSockets.js` in from GitHub, so `npm ci` needs github.com as well as the npm registry. The duel added no dependency: `packages/test-tools` now also depends on the workspace's `@pirate-sea-race/simulation`, and the smoke test also loads `@pirate-sea-race/simulation/netcode` from `dist/`, checks the web bundle for the dev-only latency setting, and plays the first frames of a practice duel on the built server.
