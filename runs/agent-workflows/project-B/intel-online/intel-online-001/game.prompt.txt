# Task: game

## Goal

Give captains something to chase besides the obvious (§3, §11). Every player starts the match with a rumor pointing at a sealed rubbing: an island, or a sector with a recovery cache. The journal keeps each lead until it goes stale, and then replaces it. Losing a rubbing in a duel gives a free fresh rumor, the comeback lead of §11. At a dock, a captain can pay 25 gold for a targeted rumor about a type they're missing. When nothing else is left, a delayed carrier sighting says roughly where another captain was seen with that type. The pure rules are on `main` (`packages/simulation/src/intel`, `docs/intel.md`). `match-online` already delivers the reveals: the final sector, the route, the minute-20 sector reveal and the minute-28 carrier reports. This feature delivers the rest (rumors, sightings, stale leads and purchases) and builds the journal.

## Design (settled)

- **Delivery:** the room delivers every intel message kind to its recipients. `revealOf` grows into a full mapping, with rumors, sightings, stale notices and `no-lead` added.
- **Journal record (room-side):** intel's `IntelPlayerState.journal` keeps island and sector rumors with their status (active, stale, resolved), but not sightings or no-lead notices, and it doesn't link a stale rumor to what followed it. So the room keeps a per-player journal log (bounded to the last 24 entries), fed by every message the player receives. Each entry has the entry number, the kind (island or sector rumor, carrier sighting, no-lead), the type, the target name, the reason, the tick, and a status of active, stale or resolved (from `rumor-resolved` when the player gets the type). A stale entry is marked superseded by the next lead of the same type the player receives, whatever its reason: intel may give a `fresh` lead instead of a `replacement` when a loss and a stale rumor coincide, or deliver a deferred sighting later. The log is part of the player's private status, so a reconnecting player gets it back.
- **Journal:** **M** opens and closes the journal panel (readable DOM text). It shows your current rumors: the type, the island name or the sector name, and why you got it (start, fresh, targeted, replacement, sighting). Stale rumors are greyed out with their replacement. It also shows the islands you know, your collected types, and your kit with its §9 description. "Islands you know" means the islands you've had in sight or moored at. The room tracks them per player from the same sight filter the markers use, keeps each island's unlocked state as last seen, and adds whether you solved its ruin. A short toast announces each new lead.
- **Sectors on the sea:** faint 3 × 3 sector borders on the sea, with the sector's name shown when you enter it. The borders only show while the journal is open or a sector lead is active, so the sea stays clean.
- **Targeted rumor (one purchase per player per tick, charge only on delivery):** moored at any dock, the journal offers "Buy a rumor (25 gold)" for each type you're missing that has an available lead (`targetedRumorAvailability`). Types with free coverage show "covered" and can't be bought. The ledger has no refunds, so no gold may ever be taken for a rumor that isn't delivered. The room resolves requests like this:
  1. **Queue with limits:** requests arrive between ticks. A request whose (player, client id) is already pending is merged with it: it takes no slot, and both are answered by the one decision. The room keeps at most 2 pending requests per player; more are refused at once (`busy`) and nothing is kept. The button is disabled while one is pending.
  2. **Replay first:** a request whose (player, client request id) the room has already decided is answered from the room's record, before any check: a bought id resends its delivered lead (the ledger's `replayed`), and a refused id resends the same refusal. The room keeps every bought id and the last 16 refusals per player, like the ledger.
  3. **One per player per tick:** each world tick, the room takes **at most one** pending request per player, oldest first; the rest wait for a later tick. So the pre-check below can't be spent twice.
  4. **Where and what:** the whole purchase (checks, `stepIntel`, `purchase`) runs in the reveal step, immediately before and around `stepIntel`, after every other ledger operation of the tick (advance, encounters, channels, settlements, grants). The checks, in order: (a) the buyer's ship is **moored at a dock on this tick** (authoritative, not just the button; a request queued at a dock and processed after casting off is refused `not-at-dock`); (b) the ledger's state (in play, not in a duel or the theft window, not disconnected, at least 25 gold), read through the ledger's own exported queries, not copied rules; (c) `targetedRumorAvailability` for that type, which must offer a lead. Any failure gives a recorded refusal with its reason, no charge, and never reaches `stepIntel`.
  5. **`stepIntel`** with the request, under a room-namespaced intel id (`<playerId>:<clientRequestId>`, because intel's ids are global and the ledger's are per player).
  6. **Only if the intel outcome delivers a lead** does the room call the ledger's `purchase` with the client's id. Steps 3 and 4(a) on the same synchronous tick make its success an invariant: no other gold changes for that player between the check and the purchase. The room doesn't write a fallback branch for it. A refused purchase here means a bug, so it throws, and the match faults (§12), like any broken invariant in `worldTick`. An integration test sends bursts of requests for different types, across players and ticks, with gold between 25 and 49, and checks every delivered targeted lead is charged exactly once and every undelivered one isn't (`checkLedger` after every tick). Any intel outcome that doesn't deliver a lead goes through **one shared branch**: a recorded refusal carrying intel's reason, with no charge. The room doesn't branch per outcome. After the checks above, few such outcomes are reachable (for example a stale rumor or a snapshot boundary on the processing tick). Test the shared branch with a reachable case, which a test hook may set up.

  Update the "Rumor flow" paragraph in `docs/objectives.md` (owned) and document the order in `docs/architecture.md`. `economy-online`'s dockside shop will later list the same purchase.
- **Keys:** **M** opens and closes the journal (the map and journal); Esc also closes it. M doesn't clash with E, R, F, J, U or I. Tab stays the browser's focus key: inside the open journal, Tab moves between its buttons, and Enter or Space activates one (§13 keyboard-navigable menus). The panel key rules apply: no key events are taken from text fields, and the helm stays live while the journal is open.
