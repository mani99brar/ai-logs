# Decisions: sea-compact

From the operator session of 2026-09-24. The operator answered the planning questions, and the assistant chose the details under their instruction to "make the ideal choice".

## Decisions

- The operator's playtest verdict: the online duel "works and it's good, can be smoother", so the combat spike passes and duel smoothness is tuned later. On the sea: "have a denser map and reduce the scale". This feature does that before `encounters-theft`, because encounters need ships that meet, and encounter tests depend on map distances.
- §3's travel times stay binding (neighbours 20–40 s, longest crossing ≤ 90 s). Shrinking the world is only legal if ships slow down, so full speed drops from 5 to 3 tiles a second, and the map shrinks to at most 112 × 112 tiles (under a quarter of today's area).
- Corrected after design challenge attempt 1. The tile's 256 sub-units, the 64 headings and `turnRate` 1 stay unchanged: 1 is already the minimum, and the claim tests depend on 256. So full speed is 39 sub-units per step (3.05 tiles/s), and handling is judged on screen: time to full speed, stopping distance, 3.2 s per full turn, and the turning circle in pixels.
- "Ships meet" becomes a check, not a hope: every neighbour route passes within sight of one central `hub` point, because shoals block the direct routes along the edges. At about 3 tiles/s, the detour through the centre also keeps neighbours inside §3's 20–40 s.
- Corrected after design challenge attempt 2. The sea keyboard dropped quick taps (it polls held keys), so it now records taps like the duel keyboard: a player-facing fix, not just test hygiene. `sail-and-dock` docks by holding ahead and E on the dock's axis, a rule already tested in the simulation, rather than timed taps. The hub disc radius is half the sight radius, so ships inside it see each other, and the check blocks the disc for every dock pair instead of rebuilding one path. The dock reach stays at 384 or more.
- Corrected after design challenge attempt 3. Driving in on the dock's axis only moors when the ship is exactly aligned: off-axis, it slides along the coast at full speed or grounds on the land beside the dock. The test now aims into a corner of the shore at a fixed heading tilted toward the land, which moors from any lateral offset, and a unit test sweeps the offsets. The out-of-sight row runs parallel to a wall with turning room, and hub sight is stated for tile centres.
- "Reduce the scale" also means drawing larger. Tiles go from 16 to about 24 px, so the 10-tile sight circle fits on the 800 × 560 view and on-screen speed stays about the same (80 → 72 px/s). The sight radius itself stays 10 tiles.
- Density is a validator, not a judgement: no navigable tile may be farther than the sight radius from a non-sea tile, and routes are funnelled through channels. This follows §3 ("Remove empty stretches before adding more sailing mechanics"), so there are no new mechanics (slow zones, currents).
- Still four small islands (§18 stage 2). A `rock` tile type for outcrops and landmarks is optional; shoals alone are acceptable.
- The flaky `sail-and-dock` browser test (seen in duel-online-fixes-001's worker phase) is fixed here, since this feature rewrites its route anyway. The final approach is in `world-task.md` item 8, the only authority on it. It must pass 5 repeats.
