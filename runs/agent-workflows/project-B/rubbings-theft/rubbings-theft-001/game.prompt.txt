# Task: game

## Goal

Close the loop that makes the game a race: **collect → fight → steal → recover**. A captain moors at an island and holds E to take its rubbing. At sea they grapple a rival and win the duel, and within 5 s they pick which of the loser's types to steal (the gold follows). A player who quits or drops for too long leaves a visible recovery cache at a dock, which others can moor at and claim. The ledger (on `main`, fully tested) holds all the rules. This feature wires it into the match room and gives players a clear, readable UI for it. After this, a match can be played for rubbings; the treasure claim follows in `objectives-endgame`.

## Design (settled)

- **The ledger per match.** A `slice` ledger is created with the match. Its sites are each regular island's dock positions: `sourcesPerIsland` (2) per island, taken from that island's docks in id order, reusing a dock if the island has fewer. The ledger's seed comes from the room's own randomness, not the public match code. **Until the ruins are wired (`objectives-endgame`), every source is unlocked at match start** (documented; the ruins feature will lock them).
- **Collecting.** Moored at a dock of island *i*, holding E runs the ledger's 6 s collection on island *i*'s first eligible source: sealed, of a type the player lacks, in the fixed type order. "Hold E to dock, keep holding to collect" is one gesture. Casting off, releasing E, entering a duel or disconnecting interrupts it, and progress resets (the ledger's rule). The HUD shows the island's sealed types (moored players only), a progress bar, and the result.
- **Duels feed the ledger.** A reservation calls `reserveDuel`. The fight's end calls `reportDuelResult` (win, draw or cancelled). **Settlement is no longer same-tick.** On a win with an eligible type, the winner gets a 5 s choice UI (the eligible types; keys 1 and 2 or a click) that sends a `choose-theft` message (validated; the winner only). `advanceLedger` applies the default at the deadline. Only when the ledger settles does the room report `settled` to the encounter rules, which starts the return. The loser sees what was taken, and both see the gold change. A draw moves nothing.
- **The world tick drives every §12 timer** (settled after design challenge attempt 1). In `advance()` the room keeps a disconnect record per player. Colyseus' `allowReconnection` is only an outer bound, 70 s: it keeps the socket slot open and decides nothing.
  - **A drop, anywhere:** `disconnectPlayer` is called at the drop. The ship stays in the world and can be grappled (the encounter rules already allow it).
  - **A drop mid-duel:** the fighter keeps the 60 s seat. The duel's 10 s absence forfeit is counted in **world ticks** by the room (not Colyseus' timer), and the loser respawns as usual. The ledger's duel forfeit and settlement handle the rubbings.
  - **Coming back before the deadline** reattaches the same player (`reconnectPlayer`).
  - **Expiry:** when the tick reaches the ledger's `reservationDeadline`, the room calls `expireReservation`. The rubbings go into a cache at the respawn dock the encounter rules would choose, and the ship leaves the world.
  - **Every other leave** (consented, or Colyseus' outer bound firing first) is a `quitPlayer`. The rubbings go into a cache at the nearest dock by sea (after any in-duel forfeit settles), and the ship leaves.
  - **No path removes a ship while the ledger still counts its player as in the match.** An invariant check in tests asserts it every tick.
- **Caches.** A cache is a marker at its dock, visible within sight. A player moored at that dock sees its contents (types) and holds E to claim one missing type in 6 s (`startCacheClaim`, then progress). An empty cache disappears.
- **What E targets at a dock with both:** first the island's sealed source of a type the player lacks, then a cache rubbing of a type they lack, each in the fixed type order. The HUD shows the current target.
- **Collecting or claiming ends protection** (§2, §6). When a collection or cache claim starts, the room passes it to the encounter rules as `protectionEndedBy` (today it's always `null`).
- **Carry-forward from the encounters-online review** (fold in):
  - **Hidden info:** a grapple telegraph's `defenderId` is blanked for viewers who can't see the defender. An integration test proves it on the raw message.
  - **Integration budget:** bring `npm run test:integration` well under 150 s. **First**, use test-only rule overrides: a short seat reservation through `createLedger({ rules })`, shorter encounter timings, and assertions in ticks. Only then consider fork parallelism, and only if the netcode harness tests (correction bounds, input delay, hash checks) stay green under it. Show the before and after durations.
  - **Test-only source layout:** a documented test-only server setting (off by default; like test placement, never read by the production entry) pins the source assignment seed or layout, so each scenario can pick an island by type.
  - **Tests:**
    - a room-level test that a moving ship stops on reservation;
    - a timing assertion for the both-not-ready cancel (at 10 s, not at the countdown's end);
    - a web unit test that the duel marker is drawn at its position with its elapsed time;
    - the `sea-duel` scenario presses attack keys like a player. Duel buttons trigger on the press (`pressed = bits & ~held`), and a held key doesn't auto-repeat in Playwright, so it taps them repeatedly. Update the acceptance wording to "taps attack keys".
  - **Arena renderer:** the match game is one Phaser game (`Phaser.AUTO`), holding both the sea and the arena. Headless Chromium renders WebGL in software, exactly the GPU-less case. So the `sea-duel` browser test records the DuelClient tick intervals during the fight and asserts their 95th percentile is under 16 ms. Switch the match game to the canvas renderer only if that fails. Update `docs/architecture.md` "Rendering" either way.
