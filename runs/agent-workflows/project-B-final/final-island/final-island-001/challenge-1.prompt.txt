## Design (settled)

- **Three sockets**, each a small islet (at most 6 × 6 tiles) with exactly one dock, all visually the same kind as other islands. The mirrored sketch may place them symmetrically. In the Tiled subset they are `final-socket` rectangles in the objects layer, with a `dock` side property; `tiledMap.ts` and `assets/source/maps/README.md` document it. They are **not** counted as regular islands (the preset's 4).
- **The claim point** of a socket is its dock tile's centre, so the §5 treasure circle (radius 4 tiles) covers navigable sea around the dock where a ship can stop. Walking on islands doesn't exist yet.
- **Validators** (each shown failing on a synthetic map in `maps.test.ts`):
  - every socket's dock is reachable by sea from every spawn slot within 90 s at full speed;
  - the three sockets lie in three **different** sectors of the 3 × 3 sector grid (the grid is computed from the map size, as the intel module does), so a sector reveal narrows the choice without giving it away;
  - the treasure circle of each socket contains at least 12 navigable tiles;
  - no socket breaks the keep-clear rule above;
  - `npm run map:build` prints each socket's sector, its worst travel time from a spawn, and its circle's navigable tile count.
- **Secret selection.** `chooseFinalSocket(map, secret)` in `seaMap.ts` is a pure function of the map and a caller-supplied **secret** (a 32-bit integer). The server will draw the secret from a cryptographic source, never from the match's public seed. The function mixes the secret non-invertibly (for example through the existing FNV-1a hash in `hashState`, or a documented integer avalanche) before reducing modulo the socket count. A test shows every socket is chosen for some secret, the choice is uniform within a tolerance over 30,000 secrets, and the same secret gives the same socket. The docs state that the match seed must never be passed as the secret.

# Decisions: final-island

From the overnight session of 2026-09-24/25. The operator is asleep and asked the assistant to decide, record each decision, and use the workflow for the code.

## Decisions

- The final island isn't a special terrain event. Three **ordinary-looking islets** are always on the map, and the server secretly picks one per match (§3's "validated hidden sockets"). There is no terrain change mid-match, so no ship can be trapped by an island appearing, and the map stays static public content (§16).
- The sockets are placed in three different sectors, so everyone learning the final island's sector (§5) narrows the hunt without giving away the exact islet.
- The claim point is the socket's dock, so the §5 treasure circle covers sea where a ship can stop, until walking on islands exists.
- The selection uses a secret drawn by the server, never the public match seed, mixed non-invertibly (§3). This is the same lesson as the ruins RNG finding.
- The feature runs in parallel with `encounters-online`, so the sockets keep clear of the hub, the spawns, the docks and the out-of-sight row the running tests use.

## Assumptions

- Socket islets use an existing island theme and look like any islet. Their final-island art comes with the reveal later.

## Deferred

- The reveal (sector to all, route to the full-set holder), the claim wiring and the server's secret draw: `objectives-endgame`.
