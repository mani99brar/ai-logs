## Design (settled after design challenge attempt 1)

- **Four sockets from one sketched islet.** Draw one islet (at most 6 × 6 tiles) in the sketched quarter, in sector column 1, for example around x 40–46, y 9–15. Then the existing mirror yields **four** sockets: two in the north-centre sector, one on each side of the x = 55 shoal wall, and two in the south-centre sector. Every sector that holds a socket holds **at least two**, so a sector reveal (§5) narrows the hunt to two candidates without giving the islet away, and all eight spawns are treated equally by symmetry.
- **No docks on sockets.** Sockets are stored as `finalSockets: { rect, claimTile }[]` on `SeaMapSource` and `SeaMapDefinition`, separate from `islands`. Their tiles are land, with **no Dock tile**, so they never enter `map.docks`: sailing, respawn, dock ids and the existing docking tests are untouched ("no gameplay change yet"). The **claim tile** is a navigable sea tile beside the islet, whose centre is the §5 claim point, so the treasure circle (radius 4) covers sea where a ship can stop.
- **Validation.**
  - `validateSeaMap` accepts socket land as belonging to a socket rectangle (not an island), and excludes sockets from the island count and the island travel-time checks.
  - New validators, each shown failing on a synthetic map in `maps.test.ts`:
    - every claim tile is reachable by sea from every spawn slot within 90 s at full speed;
    - every sector that holds a socket holds at least two;
    - each claim point's treasure circle contains at least 12 navigable tiles;
    - no socket tile, and no claim tile, lies within 8 tiles of the hub, a spawn slot, a regular dock, the out-of-sight row (3 south of the hub) or the `sail-and-dock` browser route (14 rows south of Palm Key's south dock up to that dock).
  - `npm run map:build` prints each socket's sector, its worst travel time from a spawn, and its circle's navigable tile count.
  - The 3 × 3 sector grid is computed here with a small local function over the map size. The intel module isn't on this branch, and the two must agree later: document the formula.
- **The Tiled subset** gains `final-socket` rectangles in the objects layer, each with an int `claimX` and `claimY` (the claim tile). `tiledMap.ts` and `assets/source/maps/README.md` document them.
- **Secret selection.** `chooseFinalSocket(map, secret)` in `seaMap.ts` is pure over the map and a caller-supplied 32-bit **secret**. The server will draw the secret from a cryptographic source, never from the match's public seed. The function applies a documented `Math.imul` integer avalanche to the secret before reducing it modulo the socket count. Content can't import the simulation's `hashState`. Tests:
  - every socket is chosen for some secret;
  - the choice is uniform within a tolerance over 30,000 secrets;
  - the same secret gives the same socket.
  The docs say never to pass the match seed.
