## Rules to implement

Two attack kinds share one attempt model with different content constants:

| | Sea grapple (ship → ship) | Island challenge (on foot → on foot) |
|---|---|---|
| Windup, telegraphed | 1 s | 0.6 s lunge |
| Range | `ENGAGEMENT_RANGE_TILES` (6) | 2 tiles |
| Hit area at landing | within range, and within ±4 headings (of 64) of the aim locked at the start | the same shape with its own range |
| Line of sight | required at start and at landing: an integer supercover line touching no land tile (shoals don't block) | a per-mode rule; until walking exists, nothing blocks on foot |
| Miss cooldown | 6 s | 6 s |

- **Per-mode rules.** Line of sight and the return-point rule are per-mode functions in the config. Ship mode is fully specified here. Foot mode ships with a placeholder (nothing blocks line of sight; returns stay at the player's own position) that `objectives-endgame` replaces when walking on islands exists. For foot mode, test only timing, range, cone and cooldown.
- **Integer geometry, defined once** (documented in `docs/encounters.md`):
  - *Line of sight:* a supercover line (every tile the segment between tile centres touches, both tiles at an exact corner crossing). It is symmetric by construction; the test checks both directions.
  - *Bearing:* the heading (of 64) with the largest dot product against the offset, using the existing heading table; ties go to the lower heading.
  - *Within ±N headings:* the circular heading difference is at most N.
  - *Distance 0* (overlapping ships don't block each other) counts as in range and inside every cone.
- **Starting an attempt.** A press from a player starts an attempt against a target the rules pick: the nearest eligible player of the same mode within range, line of sight and a ±8-heading front cone of the attacker's heading. Ties go to the lower stable id. A press with no such target starts nothing and costs nothing. The aim locks to the bearing toward the target at the start. Starting an attempt ends the attacker's own protection (§6).
- **Eligibility** (checked at the start and again at landing): both players are in the match (a disconnected defender can still be attacked, §12; a disconnected attacker can't start), in the same mode, not in or reserved for a duel, not respawning, and not in cooldown (attacker). The defender is unprotected. The duel cap isn't reached: at most four unresolved duels (§14). A duel counts until its settlement, and hits accepted earlier on the same tick count too. A hit refused by the cap is handled like a preemption: no cooldown.
- **Escape.** The attempt lands at the end of the windup. It hits only if the defender is still in the hit area, in line of sight and eligible. Otherwise it misses: `attempt-missed` with a reason, and the attacker's cooldown starts. An attempt whose attacker becomes ineligible during the windup (for example reserved by someone else's hit) is cancelled with no cooldown.
- **Same-tick resolution** (§5). All attempts landing on one tick resolve together, before the caller advances claims. Order the hits by attacker-to-defender distance, then attacker id, then defender id. Take each hit whose two players are both still free. A hit that loses to an earlier one is `attempt-preempted`, with no cooldown. The order players or attempts appear in the input never matters.
- **Reservation and transition.** A hit reserves both players as a duel with a stable id derived from the tick and the two ids. The transition shows a 3 s countdown. The duel starts when the countdown is over and both players are ready (the snapshot reports readiness). A player not ready 10 s after the reservation forfeits. If both are still not ready at 10 s, the duel is cancelled (both forfeit on the same tick, §12). A readiness forfeit or cancel ends the duel in the encounter state itself, with that tick as its end tick, and emits an event the caller hands to the ledger. The caller then reports only its settlement, never a second end.
- **The duel.** While a duel runs, its players are in it. The state exposes a marker per duel for other players: position and start tick for the elapsed time, and nothing else. For a fight that ran, the caller reports its end: `win` (winner), `draw`, or `cancelled` (a server fault or double forfeit), with the tick it ended. Separately, it reports the tick the ledger settled it (after the theft choice). A second end report for the same duel is refused and changes nothing.
- **Return** (§6), at settlement:
  - The winner returns at their encounter position with 8 s of protection.
  - The loser respawns 15 s after the fight ended (not after settlement), with 20 s of protection. The spawn is at a dock chosen deterministically: the dock nearest the encounter with no other active player within `SIGHT_RADIUS_TILES`, ties by dock id; if none qualifies, the dock farthest from its nearest opponent.
  - A draw or cancellation returns both players to two distinct navigable points near the encounter position, at least 3 tiles apart and within 6 tiles, with 8 s of protection.
- **Protection** has a source (`spawn`, `win`, `draw`, `respawn`) and an end tick. It ends early when the protected player starts an attempt, or when the snapshot reports that the claim or ledger ended their protection. Because §5 runs encounters before claims, the caller passes the **previous** tick's claim and ledger results. The one-tick (50 ms) lag is accepted and documented. The caller gives spawn protection at match start through the config or state.
- **Match end.** A duel still running at the deadline ends without theft (the claim code emits that event). This step only needs a way for the caller to cancel every attempt and stop new ones once the match is over.

## Decisions

- Slice 4 (`encounters-theft`) is split in two. This feature holds the pure §6 rules, like `claim-channel` and `objectives-ledger` did. `encounters-online` then wires them, the ledger and the duel into `MatchRoom`. Pure rules can be proven exhaustively (symmetry, seeded properties) before any network is involved, and they can run in parallel with `sea-compact-fixes`.
- It's based on sea-compact-001's approved candidate (`8328920`), so tests on the slice sea use the compact map that will ship.
- Where the duel runs: in the encounters slice, the duel moves into `MatchRoom` as a simulation object (§14). The standalone `DuelRoom` stays as the practice arena. This feature only emits events for it.
- Values the spec leaves open, all as content constants to tune after playtests:
  - the grapple range is `ENGAGEMENT_RANGE_TILES` (6); the island challenge range is 2 tiles;
  - the hit area is ±4 headings (±22.5°) around the aim locked at the start; target choice uses a ±8-heading (±45°) front cone;
  - the grapple miss cooldown is 6 s, the same as §6's challenge miss;
  - land blocks line of sight and shoals don't (ships see over reefs).
- The rules pick the target (nearest eligible, then lowest id) rather than the client, so a client can't aim at a hidden player (§16).
- A press with no eligible target starts nothing and costs nothing. That's forgiving for players and gives no information away. Only a real miss costs the cooldown.
- A hit that loses a same-tick conflict is "preempted" and costs no cooldown, because the attacker didn't miss.
- Readiness: the duel starts after the 3 s countdown once both clients are ready. One client not ready at 10 s forfeits; both not ready at 10 s cancels (§12's same-tick double forfeit).
- Return timing: the world return happens at ledger settlement (after the theft choice), but the loser's 15 s respawn counts from the fight's end, so the choice window doesn't lengthen it.
- The loser's respawn dock is the nearest one with no opponent within sight, falling back to the farthest from any opponent. It's deterministic and hard to camp.

- Corrected after design challenge attempt 1:
  - Line of sight and returns are per-mode. Ships are fully specified, and on foot is a placeholder until walking exists (land can't block the sight of players standing on it).
  - The geometry is defined once: a supercover line, the bearing by maximal dot product (ties low), ±N as circular difference, distance 0 inside.
  - A hit refused by the cap is a preemption (no cooldown); duels count until settlement.
  - A readiness forfeit ends the duel inside the encounter state, and the caller only reports its settlement.
  - Protection ended by a claim or collection reaches the encounter step one tick later (§5 order); that 50 ms lag is accepted.
