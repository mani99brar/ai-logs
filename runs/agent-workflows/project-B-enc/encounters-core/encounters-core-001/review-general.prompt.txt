# Decisions: encounters-core

From the overnight session of 2026-09-24. The operator is asleep and asked the assistant to decide, record each decision, and use the workflow for the code.

## Decisions

- Slice 4 (`encounters-theft`) is split in two. This feature holds the pure §6 rules, like `claim-channel` and `objectives-ledger` did. `encounters-online` then wires them, the ledger and the duel into `MatchRoom`. Pure rules can be proven exhaustively (symmetry, seeded properties) before any network is involved, and they can run in parallel with `sea-compact-fixes`.
- It's based on sea-compact-001's approved candidate (`8328920`), so tests on the slice sea use the compact map that will ship.
- Where the duel runs: in the encounters slice, the duel moves into `MatchRoom` as a simulation object (§14). The standalone `DuelRoom` stays as the practice arena. This feature only emits events for it.
- Values the spec leaves open, all as content constants to tune after playtests:
  - the grapple range is `ENGAGEMENT_RANGE_TILES` (6); the island challenge range is 2 tiles;
  - the hit area is ±4 headings (±22.5°) around the aim locked at the start; target choice uses a ±8-heading (±45°) front cone;
  - the grapple miss cooldown is 6 s, the same as §6's challenge miss;
  - land blocks line of sight and shoals don't (ships see over reefs).
- The rules pick the target (nearest eligible, then lowest id) rather than the client, so a client can't aim at a hidden player (§16).
- A press with no eligible target starts nothing and costs nothing. That's forgiving for players and gives no information away. Only a real miss costs the cooldown.
- A hit that loses a same-tick conflict is "preempted" and costs no cooldown, because the attacker didn't miss.
- Readiness: the duel starts after the 3 s countdown once both clients are ready. One client not ready at 10 s forfeits; both not ready at 10 s cancels (§12's same-tick double forfeit).
- Return timing: the world return happens at ledger settlement (after the theft choice), but the loser's 15 s respawn counts from the fight's end, so the choice window doesn't lengthen it.
- The loser's respawn dock is the nearest one with no opponent within sight, falling back to the farthest from any opponent. It's deterministic and hard to camp.

- Corrected after design challenge attempt 1:
  - Line of sight and returns are per-mode. Ships are fully specified, and on foot is a placeholder until walking exists (land can't block the sight of players standing on it).
  - The geometry is defined once: a supercover line, the bearing by maximal dot product (ties low), ±N as circular difference, distance 0 inside.
  - A hit refused by the cap is a preemption (no cooldown); duels count until settlement.
  - A readiness forfeit ends the duel inside the encounter state, and the caller only reports its settlement.
  - Protection ended by a claim or collection reaches the encounter step one tick later (§5 order); that 50 ms lag is accepted.
