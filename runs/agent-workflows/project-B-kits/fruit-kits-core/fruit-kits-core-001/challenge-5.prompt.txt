# Task: kits

## Goal

Give the duel its first two Devil-fruit kits, **Ember** and **Smoke** (§9), in the pure duel simulation and its move data. Eating a fruit replaces the Duelist's two specials. Normals, throw, guard and Haki stay the same. The kits must feel distinct and fair:

- **Smoke** changes spacing and hides intent with a feint dash and a smoke screen, but it hits softly and its reappearance is readable.

The fruitless Duelist must stay a real choice against both. This feature builds and proves the kits. `fruit-kits-online` then brings pedestals, eating and the arena drawing.

## Design (settled after design challenge attempt 1)

- **Kit data stays inside the versioned moveset.** All moves live in the one `moves` list. `DuelMoveset` gains `kits: { duelist, ember, smoke }`, each holding its `special1` and `special2` move ids. `moveset.commands` stays the Duelist's (DuelRoom reads `commands.throw`). So `movesetVersion` covers every kit, and a state can never be stepped with different kit data. Each fighter's kit id lives in `FighterState`: hashed, snapshotted and checked by `restoreDuel`. The step reads a fighter's specials from `moveset.kits[fighter.kit]`. `DuelConfig`'s shape doesn't change.
- **Moves keep their shape.** `hitbox: Box` stays required on every move (`apps/web/src/arenaScene.ts` depends on it). Non-striking behaviour is expressed with optional sub-objects (`projectile?`, `zone?`, `cancel?`), not a union without a hitbox.
- **Which moves need what:**
  - The rising flare is an ordinary strike with strike invulnerability during its startup, like the counter slash.
  - The feint is an attack-action move whose `advance` and `strikeInvulnerable` (at most its startup) give the dash, then a recovery of at least N frames (the readable reappearance).
  - Only the bolt and the screen add state, as **fixed per-fighter slots** in `FighterState` (`bolt: Bolt | null`, `zone: Zone | null`). "At most one per fighter" then holds by construction, `restoreDuel`'s existing fighter check validates them, and swapping fighters carries them along. No new `FighterAction` values and no general entity system, which would break the renderer's switches.
  - Each move has one explicit `effect` field: `strike` (the default), `projectile`, `zone` or `feint`. The schema requires the `projectile` data exactly when `effect` is `projectile`, and the `zone` data exactly when it is `zone`. One exported helper, `strikesWithHitbox(move)` (meaning `effect === "strike"`), is the only striking rule. The step, the start-gap reach check and later the renderer use it. The bolt cast, the screen cast and the feint don't strike: the feint deals no damage.
- **Contact rules for bolts** (one resolution function turns bolt overlaps into the step's existing `Contact`):
  - Melee takes priority: if a fighter is hit by melee and a bolt on the same frame, only the melee contact applies, and the bolt dies.
  - A bolt counts as a strike against a grab, so a bolt landing on the same frame beats the grab.
  - To block a bolt, hold the direction the bolt is travelling (away from the side it comes from), not away from its owner, so a cross-up can never make a bolt unblockable.
  - Bolt contacts are swept, by substeps: each frame's movement is split into substeps no longer than half the smallest box width involved, and overlap is tested at each substep with both bolts moved in step. Two bolts closing fast, or a bolt and a hurtbox, can never pass through each other, and two bolts moving apart never falsely meet.
  - Every pairwise resolution (bolt meets bolt, melee priority, bolt beats grab, zone marking) is symmetric: neither fighter is processed first.
  - Strike-invulnerable frames also ignore bolts.
  - Two bolts that meet destroy each other.
  - A bolt hit never pushes its owner back from the wall.
  - The bolt is a `mid` (a crouch doesn't dodge it) with its own move id. §8's projectile row values (22 startup, 30 recovery) are floors, written into the data.
- **Cancels:** the feint cancels only into normals (light, heavy, throw), never into another feint or a special. The cancel opens only in its recovery, **after the first `FEINT_REAPPEARANCE_FRAMES` (12) frames**, so every feint, cancelled or not, shows its readable reappearance (Smoke's vulnerability in §9). Strike invulnerability ends with the dash and never covers the reappearance.
- **Entity moves:** the bolt's strike data (box, speed, life, guard height, damage, hitstun, blockstun) lives inside the cast move's `projectile` sub-object, with an id used only in events. `restoreDuel` accepts an event naming a projectile id only if it belongs to the attacker's kit, and it refuses a fighter whose `moveId` is outside its kit's specials and the shared normals. The schema requires `commands.special1/2` to equal `kits.duelist`'s. The bolt is not a second move in `moves`, so it can never be a fighter's `moveId`. A move with `projectile` or `zone` data never strikes with its own hitbox. The melee start-gap reach check covers only moves that strike. A bolt contact never marks the owner's current move as having hit, and never pushes the owner.
- **Anti-air:** the duel has no air attacks, so "punishes jumps" means the flare hits a jumping fighter at heights and ranges where the universal anti-air misses. Its startup invulnerability also makes it a grounded reversal, and its long whiff recovery is the price.
- **"Within budget"** means named checks written down in `docs/fruit-kits.md`: the bolt's startup is at least 22 and its recovery at least 30 (§8's projectile row); every other new special starts up in at least 8 frames (the Duelist's fastest special); the flare's recovery is at least 30; the feint's recovery is at least its 12 reappearance frames; on-block advantage is consistent with blockstun.

## Acceptance

Each item names the test that proves it. Tests live beside the code they cover.

1. **Kit data.** `@pirate-sea-race/content/moves` exports the Duelist, Ember and Smoke kits: each kit's two special commands and its moves, validated by the schema. A duel is created with one kit per fighter (default Duelist). Content tests:

   - each new special is within §8's budgets;
   - an invalid kit (an unknown move id, a projectile move without projectile data, a zone move without zone data) is refused.
2. **Ember** (U: flame bolt, I: rising flare).
   - The flame bolt is a projectile entity: it travels, is blocked or hits, dies on hit, at the arena wall or at the end of its life, and at most one is alive per fighter.
   - The rising flare beats a jump-in (anti-air) but has long recovery on a whiff.
   - Scripted duel tests:
     - the bolt hits at range and is blockable;
     - the flare's hitbox reaches above the universal anti-air's top edge, so it hits a jumping fighter near the jump apex where the anti-air misses (the anti-air's data is unchanged);
     - a whiffed flare is punishable by a Duelist heavy;
     - a Duelist forward-jumping over a bolt from mid range lands a heavy on the Ember before it recovers;
     - a second bolt fired as early as possible after a bolt hit can be blocked (no bolt loop);
     - all the contact rules above: melee priority, bolt beats grab, block direction on a cross-up, invulnerability, bolt meets bolt, no wall pushback for the owner.
3. **Smoke** (U: feint dash, I: smoke screen).
   - The feint dash is a short, cancellable dash with a readable end.
   - The smoke screen is a timed zone entity. It deals no damage, and the simulation marks the fighters inside it so the renderer can obscure their startup animations while still showing position and health.
   - Scripted tests:
     - the feint changes spacing and can cancel into a normal;
     - the screen expires on time;
     - Smoke's specials deal no damage: its offence is the shared normals, which is §9's "low direct damage";
     - the reappearance after a feint is at least N frames long (a named constant);
     - feint → feint and feint → special are refused, a cancel before the 12th reappearance frame is refused, and a cancelled feint still shows all 12 reappearance frames without invulnerability.
4. **The Duelist mirror is unchanged.** Before changing the step, run a throwaway recorder from outside the repository (for example in `/tmp`) at the base commit. It generates the data block of `packages/simulation/src/duel/mirror.test.ts`, the test file itself, which holds for each of three seeded random-input Duelist-mirror duels:
   - a per-frame hash of the projected legacy fields (every `FighterState` field that exists today, plus the frame's events, phase and result);
   - a full JSON-string checkpoint every 300 frames, for diagnosing a mismatch.

   It must stay about 200 KB, with no `fs`, no JSON imports and no tsconfig change. Keeping the data in the `*.test.ts` file keeps it out of `dist` (`tsconfig.build.json` excludes `src/**/*.test.ts`). Choose the seeds and the input mix (include guarding) so that the three duels together cover at least 5,000 frames before their results. The test shows the new code reproduces every hash: the version and the new kit and slot fields aside.
5. **Matchups.** For each pair, including the mirrors (Duelist vs Ember, Duelist vs Smoke, Ember vs Smoke, Ember vs Ember, Smoke vs Smoke):
   - a scripted exchange shows each kit's stated advantage and vulnerability from §9's table;
   - a seeded random-input property test over at least 1,800 frames never breaks the duel invariants (health and stamina bounds, one action per fighter, at most one bolt and one zone each), and replays to the same hash. The mixed pairs also get a scripted exchange showing each kit's stated advantage and vulnerability from §9's table.
6. **Snapshots round-trip.** Every state of every kit property run (item 5) round-trips through `snapshotDuel` → JSON → `restoreDuel` to the same hash, including bolt-contact frames (the online client restores the server's snapshots every 6 frames). A **symmetry test** swaps the two fighters in scripted bolt, screen and feint exchanges and gets the mirrored outcome.
7. **Rollback.** Snapshot and restore mid-projectile and mid-smoke-screen, then replay, gives the same hash as the straight run (the existing rollback test pattern).
8. **Docs.** `docs/fruit-kits.md` (about a page): the kits, their frame data, the new entities, and what the renderer must show.
9. `npm ci` then `npm run typecheck`, `npm run test:unit`, `npm run test:integration` and `npm run build` pass from a clean checkout.
